Peter McEchron Cornwell passed away peacefully in his Sleepy Hollow home on October 22, 2024 at the age of 77.  He lived in Fairfax for 7 years and Sleepy Hollow for more than 40 years. 

Peter was born in 1946 in Glen Falls, NY to Kirkham and Margaret Cornwell.  They married in a “war wedding” in 1943 while Kirkham was a flying instructor for the Navy aviation school. Later, Kirkham was a small businessman and the son of the illustrator and muralist Dean Cornwell.  

Peter went to St Lawrence College in upstate NY where he was part of the US Army’s Reserve Officers Training Corps, with the expectation of deployment to Vietnam as a forward observer. At his pre-deployment physical, the military doctor diagnosed Peter with a malignant tumor in his thyroid, which led to a medical discharge.  

Thyroid cancer at age 22 proved to be the first of three serious health events through Peter’s life. Through each, he showed incredible determination, vitality, and a powerful will to recover.

After his army discharge and successful thyroid surgery, Peter did what young people did in 1969–he moved to San Francisco. 

The year was 1969. In San Francisco, Peter forged a new life, met his future wife, Nancy Cornwell nee Perry.  This was their first house on 47th Avenue.

Peter and Nancy moved to Fairfax in 1976 and started a family the year afterward.  

In 1977, I was born, which you can see evidence of here.  I don’t consciously remember these moments, but I can still remember the love that echoed from them.  My brother was born in 1979.  Neither of us really ever achieve Dad’s constant state of tanness, even to this day.  

Coaching, baseball, and tennis were a consistent presence in Peter’s adult life. He was often found on Marin courts or fields, playing, coaching or chatting on the sidelines. In his later years, he was a doubles specialist and his quick hands on half volleys were often remarked upon.  

Peter loved coaching youth sports (baseball and tennis), as well as Special Olympics (tennis). and was a lifelong mentor through the Big Brothers and Big Sisters program. This is Jason Browne, Dad’s “little brother” for whom he was a mentor through the teenage years.  
In 1988, Peter developed a re-occurring insulin producing tumors in his pancreas. This condition required three major operations, culminating in the removal of his pancreas. But he recovered with characteristic vitality.

After a remarkable recovery from this second major health event, in the 2000’s Peter and Nancy began spending summers in Grand Lake Stream, Maine, and Peter developed a passion for fishing, with a special fondness for land-locked salmon. For many years Peter drove across the country twice a year, accompanied by Nancy and/or a faithful canine companion. Peter and Nancy maintained an off-grid cabin in Maine for many years as well as many decades-long friendships.

 Peter’s love of Maine, his boat and fishing in West Grand Lake was contagious.  He took his grandchildren fishing and would blow his air horn whenever they caught a salmon, to their huge excitement.  He enjoyed taking them in his boat to jump off Caribou rock in the middle of the lake, as well as to various beaches for picnicking and swimming.  We all enjoyed our blueberry pancake breakfasts together;  Peter would cook them on a griddle with blueberries freshly picked by his grandchildren and their parents. 

In 2019, Peter suffered the third major health event of his life–a severe heart attack while in Grand Lake Stream. He did, however, in characteristic style, outlive the five-year prognosis he was given after the heart attack.
