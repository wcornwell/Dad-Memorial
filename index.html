Peter McEchron Cornwell

1946 – 2024

Glens Falls

Peter was born in 1946 in Glens Falls, New York, to Kirkham and Margaret Cornwell. His grandfather, Dean Cornwell, was a celebrated illustrator and muralist. In one painting that has since surfaced on an art history channel, a young boy holds a musket as Betsy Ross sews the first American flag. That boy was Peter.

He grew up playing baseball, basketball, and tennis in a small town where sport was the common language of community. That engagement — competitive, generous, unhurried — would remain a thread through the rest of his life.

St. Lawrence

Peter attended St. Lawrence College in upstate New York, where he enrolled in the Army's Reserve Officers' Training Corps expecting to serve as a forward observer in Vietnam. At his pre-deployment physical, a military doctor found a malignant tumor in his thyroid. He received a medical discharge and, after surgery, recovered fully.

It was the first of three serious health events he would face across his life. Through each, his response was the same: focus, patience, and an almost stubborn refusal to stop moving forward.

San Francisco

After his discharge, Peter did what young people did in 1969 — he moved to San Francisco. He met Nancy Perry there; they married and made their first home on 47th Avenue. In 1976 they crossed the bay to Fairfax, where Will was born in 1977 and Charley in 1979. In 1985, Peter founded Angio West, Inc., a medical device distributor that supplied hospitals across the Bay Area for more than twenty years.

Coaching was a constant alongside his work — youth baseball, tennis, Special Olympics. He was a longtime mentor through Big Brothers and Big Sisters. He was a fixture on Marin courts and fields, and in his later years became a doubles specialist with quick hands at the net that other players couldn't match.

Maine

In the early 2000s, Peter and Nancy began spending summers in Grand Lake Stream, Maine. He developed a deep affection for land-locked salmon and West Grand Lake, making the cross-country drive twice a year — often with Nancy, sometimes with a dog alongside. They maintained an off-grid cabin and friendships that had grown over decades.

He took his grandchildren fishing and blew his air horn every time they caught a salmon. He drove them out to Caribou Rock to jump into the lake, and cooked blueberry pancakes on a griddle with berries the grandchildren had picked that morning. In 2019, he suffered a severe heart attack while on the lake and was airlifted to Bangor. He outlived the five-year prognosis that followed.

October 22, 2024
Peter McEchron Cornwell passed away peacefully at his home
in Sleepy Hollow, California, at the age of 77.

He is survived by his wife Nancy, his children Will and Charley,
his siblings Kirk and Lucy, and five grandchildren.

Memorial events will be held in Marin, Grand Lake Stream,
and Glens Falls in the months ahead.
